\title{\vspace{-0.9cm}\LARGE\bfseries Quantum sampling for a long/short stock split:\\\Large a same-budget benchmark of QAOA variants on 12--29 qubits}
\author{Oussama Turki and Tushar Pandey (Team ZollerQu)\\\small Qollab $\times$ IonQ Hackathon, 9--11 October 2026\\\small This effort is supported by Qollab \& IonQ.}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}\noindent
We encode the choice of a low-risk long/short (or buy/skip) stock portfolio as an Ising problem with one qubit per asset, enumerate the exact optimum at 12 assets, and benchmark seven circuit families (linear-ramp QAOA, free-angle QAOA with five optimisers, CVaR, multi-angle, counterdiabatic, two imaginary-time methods and a constraint-preserving XY mixer) on an exact statevector simulator against classical heuristics \emph{under the same budget}: 1\,000 measured shots versus 1\,000 evaluated candidate portfolios. Tuned circuits find the optimum in 99--100\,\% of runs, restarted greedy descent in 82 and 100\,\% (modes A and B), a 1\,000-step annealing run in 47 and 100\,\%, random sampling in 22\,\%. The circuits, however, first cost 300--10\,700 offline simulated circuits to tune, a price the heuristics do not pay, and at 12 qubits 1\,000 greedy evaluations already cover a quarter of the search space. Three findings are robust and measured: the linear-ramp recipe as commonly written has the wrong sign for the $R_x(+2\beta)$ mixer and steers towards the worst portfolios; angles tuned at 12 qubits transfer to 16, 20 and 29 qubits without loss of sampling quality; and all-to-all connectivity saves a factor 4.3 (12 qubits) to 6.5 (29 qubits) in two-qubit gates per layer against a heavy-hex grid (66 vs.\ 285, 244 vs.\ 1\,576). At 29 qubits, where an exact simulation gives the true optimum, the untuned circuit samples far above random (quality 0.96 and 0.98) and finds portfolios of the same quality as the heuristics within the same budget, but the heuristics reach the exact optimum more often; we make no performance claim at this size. All optimised portfolios reduced out-of-sample risk; none beat the market's return. No quantum advantage is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and data}
Each asset $i$ gets a spin $s_i\in\{+1,-1\}$. Mode~A (long/short): $+1$ = buy, $-1$ = short, weights $s_i/n$. Mode~B (buy/skip): $x_i=(1+s_i)/2\in\{0,1\}$, weight $x_i/n$, the rest in cash. With training-window mean weekly log returns $\mu$ and sample covariance $C$, the score to minimise is
\begin{equation}
J = \frac{w^{\mathsf T} C\, w}{V_{\mathrm{ref}}} - \eta\,\frac{\mu^{\mathsf T} w}{\sigma_{\mathrm{ref}}},\qquad V_{\mathrm{ref}}=\overline{\operatorname{diag} C},\; \sigma_{\mathrm{ref}}=\sqrt{V_{\mathrm{ref}}},
\end{equation}
with $\eta\in\{0.25,0.5,1,2\}$ ($\eta=1$ default) and an optional balance constraint (equal numbers on each side). $J$ is an Ising energy $\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}s_is_j+\sum_i h_i s_i+c$ exactly (verified to $10^{-16}$ on random states); thinning the 29-asset couplings to the strongest 60\,\% by $|\rho_{ij}|$ gives a surrogate within 0.013 of the true score (rank correlation 0.975), and every candidate is re-scored on the dense objective.

\textbf{Data.} Kaggle ``Huge Stock Market Dataset'' (CC0): 29 named US assets in 10 groups (tech, gold \& silver, energy, banks, bonds, healthcare, consumer staples, utilities, real estate, copper) plus SPY as a market reference, weekly closes 2006-05-26 to 2017-11-10 (599 weeks, 598 weekly log returns). Chronological split: 418 training weeks (to 2014-05-30, containing the 2008 crash) and 180 held-out weeks; nothing was chosen with test data. The 12-asset instance takes one asset per group in a fixed round-robin: AAPL, GLD, XOM, JPM, TLT, JNJ, PG, XLU, VNQ, FCX, MSFT, SLV. Bonds are the only group negatively correlated with the rest (mean $\rho\approx-0.22$; TLT--XLK $-0.45$); gold is near zero ($+0.10$ to $+0.14$). An earlier thinning rule by $|C_{ij}|$ was dropped because it removed every bond link.

\section{Methods}
\textbf{Answer key and classical baselines.} At 12 assets all $2^{12}=4\,096$ portfolios are enumerated (exactly one optimum per setting). Baselines, written in NumPy: uniform random sampling, greedy single-flip (or swap) descent with restarts, and simulated annealing. Balance is enforced by a penalty $\lambda(\sum_i s_i)^2$ at 12 assets, with $\lambda$ verified by enumeration so that no unbalanced state beats the best balanced one, and by a hard constraint in the solvers at 29.

\textbf{Circuit.} $H^{\otimes n}$, then per layer $k$: $R_{zz}(2\gamma_k J_{ij}/\text{scale})$ on every kept link, $R_z(2\gamma_k h_i/\text{scale})$ on every asset, $R_x(2\beta_k)$ on every qubit, with one common scale $=\max(|J_{ij}|,|h_i|)$; the constant is a global phase. Linear-ramp QAOA (LR-QAOA)~\cite{lrqaoa} uses $\gamma_k=\tfrac{k}{p}\Delta\gamma$, $\beta_k=(1-\tfrac{k-1}{p})\Delta\beta$ and was grid-tuned (300 points per $p$, $p=1..5$) by lowest expected energy, never by the optimum's probability, because the optimum is unknown in a real problem. Free-angle QAOA ($2p$ angles) was tuned by COBYLA, Nelder--Mead, Powell, L-BFGS-B and our own SPSA from the same five starts (the default ramp, the tuned ramp, three fixed-seed random points) with a cap of $200p$ circuit evaluations each. Extras at the same cap: a CVaR objective ($\alpha=0.1,0.25$), multi-angle QAOA ($3p$: separate angles for links and fields), a first-order counterdiabatic $R_y$ layer (DC-QAOA, cf.~\cite{kipu}), VarQITE angle training (McLachlan, central differences), varQITE with the Pauli-term update rule of IonQ/ORNL~\cite{varqite} (ansatz A: $R_y$ + CX per link, 66 parameters; ansatz B: $R_{zz}$ per link + $R_x$ layer; implemented from the method description, no code reused), and an XY mixer with a Dicke initial state for the balance constraint. An independent NumPy statevector simulator (1.7\,ms per $p=2$ circuit at 12 qubits) agrees with Qiskit~2.5.2 \texttt{Statevector} to $3\times10^{-16}$ for all variants; the $R_{zz}/R_z$ sign convention is unit-tested against the explicit diagonal unitary. 122 unit tests pass; the full benchmark ran in 121\,s on 8 cores.

\textbf{Same-budget protocol.} One \emph{candidate evaluation} is the energy of one portfolio: a measured shot, a random sample, one neighbour examined by greedy descent, or one annealing proposal. Every method gets exactly $B=1\,000$ of them; hit@1000 is the probability that the optimum is found within the budget (exact $1-(1-P_{\mathrm{opt}})^{1000}$ for circuits and confirmed by 200 sampled repetitions; 200 repetitions for the heuristics). Greedy restarts from random states until the budget is used (19 and 12 restarts on average in modes A and B); annealing runs one geometric schedule of 1\,000 proposals. The circuits' offline tuning cost is listed separately.

Table~1 and Fig.~\ref{fig:budget} give the fair comparison. With 1\,000 evaluations each, the tuned circuits reach the optimum in 99--100\,\% of runs in both modes; restarted greedy reaches 82\,\% (A) and 100\,\% (B), the 1\,000-step annealer 47\,\% and 100\,\%, uniform sampling 22\,\%. Three caveats keep this honest: (i) the circuits were first tuned with 300 (LR-QAOA) to 10\,685 (free-angle, all optimisers and starts) simulated circuits, which the heuristics never needed; (ii) at 12 qubits 1\,000 greedy evaluations already cover a quarter of the 4\,096 states, and the annealer's schedule was not tuned for such a short run; (iii) all distributions are exact and noiseless. Mode~B is easy for every method because its optimum (``buy AAPL and TLT'', everything else in cash) is reached by few flips from most states; mode~A, a genuine hedge, separates the methods.

\section{Per-shot results and the two findings}
Per shot, the tuned circuits are $30\times$ (A) and $85\times$ (B) more likely than uniform sampling to return the optimum (Table~1); the CVaR objective reaches $236\times$ in mode~B at a lower mean quality. In mode~A the circuit's single most likely portfolio \emph{is} the optimum (rank 1 of 4\,096: long AAPL, MSFT, GLD, SLV, JPM, TLT, PG, XLU; short XOM, JNJ, VNQ, FCX; Fig.~\ref{fig:cut}); in mode~B it is ``buy TLT only'', one asset from the optimum (rank 6).

\textbf{Finding 1: the sign of the ramp.} With the mixer $R_x(+2\beta)$ and $H^{\otimes n}$ as the initial state, lowering the energy requires $\gamma$ and $\beta$ of \emph{opposite} sign. The recipe as commonly written ($\Delta\gamma=0.6$, $\Delta\beta=0.3$) drives the state towards the \emph{highest} energies: quality 0.45 at $p=5$ against 0.81 for uniform sampling in mode~A, 0.33 against 0.71 in mode~B, and the probability of the worst portfolio grows with $p$. Fig.~\ref{fig:grid} shows the grid: the good region lies entirely in the half-plane $\Delta\beta<0$. All tuned ramps have $\Delta\beta<0$: A (0.3,$-$0.3), (0.5,$-$0.3), (0.7,$-$0.4), (0.7,$-$0.4), (0.7,$-$0.4) for $p=1..5$; B (1.0,$-$0.7), (1.4,$-$0.6), (1.4,$-$0.6), (1.4,$-$0.5), (1.3,$-$0.5).

\textbf{Finding 2: angle transfer.} The ramp tuned at 12 assets keeps its quality at 16 and 20 assets (Table~\ref{tab:transfer}), within 0.005 of a fresh grid search at 16, and still beats uniform sampling at 20 assets thinned to 60\,\% of the links as the 29-asset model is. This justifies running the 29-qubit circuits with the 12-asset ramp, untuned; beyond about 20 qubits offline tuning is impossible and would have to be paid in hardware shots.

\textbf{Optimisers and extras.} The optimiser matters less than the start: from the tuned ramp all five reach about the same quality (mean over starts and $p$: A 0.857--0.895, B 0.950--0.969), random starts often stall, and L-BFGS-B needs the fewest evaluations (24--98). CVaR raises $P_{\mathrm{opt}}$ at the cost of mean quality; multi-angle gains little; the counterdiabatic layer in mode~B concentrates on near-optimal states (quality 0.985) but never on the optimum. VarQITE angle training matches standard QAOA from the same starts. varQITE with the Pauli-term update (Fig.~\ref{fig:varqite}) lowers the energy steadily but slowly at the published learning rates (0.05--0.4) and reaches quality 0.880 (A) / 0.871 (B) after 60 steps and 7\,980 simulated circuits per run with 66 two-qubit gates, below LR-QAOA at one layer; its IonQ-native ansatz~B is a stationary point at $\theta=0$ (every parameter-shift derivative vanishes) and needs a random start. Exact imaginary-time evolution of a diagonal cost is classical Boltzmann sampling; as a reference curve it overtakes the best LR-QAOA at $\tau=80$ (A) / 160 (B).

The penalty coupling $2\lambda$ is $12\times$ (A) / $5\times$ (B) the largest objective term, so penalised circuits mostly learn the constraint and their grids run into the small-$\Delta\gamma$ edge. The XY mixer (12 XX+YY gates per layer on a ring, Dicke state prepared generically, not gate-optimised) keeps every shot balanced and is $10\times$ more likely than the penalty to return the best balanced portfolio (Table~\ref{tab:balance}). At 29 qubits post-selection of the unconstrained circuit is the practical choice.

\section{Out-of-sample finance}
Over the 180 held-out weeks (simple returns, equal weights, no costs), the classically optimal portfolios of all 32 settings (2 modes $\times$ 4 $\eta$ $\times$ balance on/off) had lower weekly volatility than SPY (0.00--0.91\,\% vs.\ 1.57\,\%) and than 1\,000 random portfolios of the same kind; 14 of 32 earned more than a random portfolio; none beat SPY's return ($+0.165$ vs.\ $+0.214$\,\% per week in a strongly rising market). The method reduces risk; it does not raise return. Trading costs, borrow fees and slippage are not modelled, and the universe has survivorship bias.

\section{Limitations}
All results are exact, noiseless calculations. The same-budget comparison favours the circuits only after their offline tuning, which is impossible beyond about 20 qubits; the angle transfer is the way around it and is measured exactly up to 29 qubits, where the circuit keeps its sampling quality but the classical heuristics reach the exact optimum more often. At 12 qubits the problem is small enough that 1\,000 greedy evaluations cover a quarter of the search space. The financial evaluation models no costs, borrow fees or slippage, and the asset universe has survivorship bias. Not attempted: warm starts, variational real-time evolution, recursive QAOA. Code, 126 tests, the saved angles and all run records (including every unhelpful variant) are in the team repository.
